Pelican Bay Fees: Here's Exactly What You're
Paying and What You're Actually Getting
By Jake Gunther -February 02, 2026
Blog Home (/Blog)Moving To Naples (/Blog/Search/Category-Moving To Naples)
Gen Z Lens (/Blog/Search/Category-Gen Z Lens)Pelican Bay (/Blog/Search/Category-Pelican Bay)
Let's be real. One of the first things people ask when they look at a condo in Pelican Bay is: "Wait, how much are
the fees?" And honestly? That's the right question to ask. No one should buy into a community without fully
understanding what their money is going toward every single month. So here it is - no fluff, no spin. Just the
numbers and what they get you.
We're going to use a real listing as our example: a 3-bedroom condo at The Crescent in Pelican Bay — 8456
Abbington Circle. (https://linktr.ee/8456_Abbington_1722)My colleagues Jane Darling and Melinda Gunther have
the full listing details over on their site, but for this post, we're zeroing in on one thing: the cost of living here.
Actually living here.
The Two Fee Buckets You Need to Know About
Pelican Bay isn't like most Naples neighborhoods where you just pay one HOA and call it a day. Here, there are two
layers of fees, and they cover two very different things. Once you understand the split, it all makes a lot more
sense.
Layer 1 is your building's condo association fee. That fee is specific to The Crescent and covers everything related
to your actual building and immediate surroundings.
Layer 2 is the Pelican Bay Foundation fee. That's the community-wide fee that covers all the big stuff the whole
neighborhood shares.
Both are mandatory. Both are worth it. Let's break them down.

Layer 1: The Crescent Condo Fee — $4,254 Per
(cid:0)
Quarter
That's roughly $1,418 a month. Here's what's included:
Cable TV — already in the fee. No separate bill (provider is Hotwire)
Internet and WiFi — also covered. You're not paying a separate Comcast or AT&T bill for this.
Building Insurance — this is a big one. Your condo association's policy covers the exterior of the building and
common areas. You'd still want an HO-6 policy for your personal belongings and interior finishes, but the bulk of the
building coverage? Handled.
Irrigation Water — the landscaping around the building stays green. You're not thinking about it. You're not paying
for it separately.
Landscaping — same idea. The grounds around The Crescent are maintained professionally. It just looks good, all
the time.
Exterior Pest Control — bugs in Florida are real. This is taken care of at the building level.
Trash Removal — covered.
Pool Maintenance — the pool is maintained and ready to use. No one's asking you to pitch in.
Gate Maintenance — The Crescent is gated. That gate system, the cameras, the access - it's all maintained through
this fee.
So when you look at that $4,254 quarterly number, you're not just paying for a place to live. You're paying for cable,
internet, insurance coverage, landscaping, pest control, trash, pool, and security, all bundled into one payment. If
you added all of that up separately, you'd be paying more. A lot more.
Layer 2: Pelican Bay Foundation Fee — $3,295
Per Year + $10,000 One-Time Transfer Fee
This is the community-wide fee. It's what gives you access to everything that makes Pelican Bay different from
every other neighborhood in Naples.
The $10,000 transfer fee is a one-time cost paid at closing. It's not annual. It's not recurring. You pay it once when
you buy, and it goes toward maintaining the community's infrastructure and shared spaces.
The $3,295 annual fee is what covers the big-ticket amenities. And by big-ticket, here's what we're talking about:
Private beach access — Pelican Bay has close to three miles of Gulf-front beach that is exclusively for residents.
Beach attendants set up your chairs and umbrellas. There are beachside restaurants where you can eat lunch with
your feet basically in the sand. This alone is worth the fee to a lot of people.
Beach tram service — you don't have to drive or even walk to the beach if you don't want to. The tram takes you
there.

Tennis — nearly 20 Har-Tru courts with professional instruction available. Whether you play every day or once a
month, it's there.

Pickleball — the courts are brand new. If you play, you already know this is a big deal.
Fitness and Wellness Center — a legitimate, state-of-the-art facility. Not a gym with a couple of treadmills. A real
fitness center with a wellness component built in.
Gulf-front dining — there are two restaurants right on the beach, run by the community. Casual lunch spots and
nicer dinner options. You don't have to leave Pelican Bay to eat well. There are also beachfront bars to network and
enjoy the sunset.
Nature trails and boardwalks — miles of walking and biking paths through preserved mangroves and along Clam
Bay. It's genuinely beautiful here.
So What Does It Actually Cost to Live Here?
The Real Math.
Let's put it all on the table for one year:
Fee Frequency Annual Cost
Crescent Condo Fee $4,254/quarter $17,016
Pelican Bay Foundation Fee Once/year $3,295
Total Annual Fees $20,311
Plus the $10,000 one-time transfer fee at closing — but that's a one-and-done.
That breaks down to about $1,692 a month in total fees. For that, you get cable, internet, building insurance,
landscaping, pest control, trash, pool, a gated entrance, private beach access with tram service, tennis, pickleball, a
fitness center, beachside restaurants, and nature trails.
For context — a gym membership alone in Naples can run $200 to $400 a month. A beach club membership? Easily
$5,000 to $10,000 a year or more. Cable and internet together? Another $200 a month, at least. When you start
stacking up what's included, $1,692 a month for all of it starts to feel less like a fee and more like a deal.
What You Don't Get for Free (The Stuff People
Forget to Ask About)
Being transparent here, because there are a few things that aren't covered by these fees and you should budget for
them:
Property taxes — Florida has no state income tax, but you do pay property taxes based on assessed value. For
Pelican Bay condos, this is something to factor in when you're calculating your total monthly cost.
HO-6 Insurance — as mentioned earlier, the condo association covers the building exterior. You still need your own
policy for your personal property and interior finishes. Expect to pay for this separately.
Utilities — water (non-irrigation) and electricity are not included in the condo fee. These are your responsibility.

Golf — the golf course in Pelican Bay is not included in the Foundation fee. Membership is separate and currently
closed to new members.

The Bottom Line
Pelican Bay fees aren't cheap. Let's not pretend they are. But they're not arbitrary either. Every dollar is tied to
something real, something you can see, use, and enjoy. Private beaches, maintained grounds, a gated community,
resort-style amenities. It's all there, and it's all funded through these fees.
The key is understanding exactly what you're paying for before you buy. No surprises. No guessing. Just clear
information and a solid decision.
